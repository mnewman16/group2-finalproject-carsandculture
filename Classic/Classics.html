Classics

Porsche

Porsche is a German performance powerhouse founded in 1931, best known for the legendary 91and its "evolution over revolution" design philosophy. The brand has successfully expanded from pure sports cars into luxury SUVs like the Macan and Cayenne, which now lead their sales. Today, Porsche is at the forefront of the electric transition, offering high-voltage performance through the Taycan and the first-ever 911 hybrid models.

Image via Broad Arrow Auctions

Jaguar

Jaguar's classic legacy is defined by the slogan "Grace, Space, and Pace," a philosophy that produced some of the most beautiful and technologically advanced cars of the 20th century. The brand's golden era was anchored by the E-Type, famously hailed by Enzo Ferrari as the most beautiful car ever made, and the dominant C-Type and D-Type racers that pioneered disc brakes and aerodynamic unibody design at Le Mans. Beyond sports cars, Jaguar invented the modern sport sedan with models like the Mark 2, which combined an opulent, leather-and-wood interior with the high-performance straight-six engine of a racer.

Image via Hemmings

Honorable Mentions

American muscle cars, led by the Ford Mustang and Dodge Charger, defined an era of raw power with their massive V8 engines and aggressive, straight-line speed. In Europe, the Mercedes-Benz 300SL Gullwing and the Ferrari 250 GTO became the gold standard for luxury and racing prestige, combining advanced engineering with timeless, sculptural designs. Japan’s classic icons, such as the Toyota 2000GT and the early Nissan Skyline GT-R, proved that precision and reliability could compete with the world’s best sports cars. Together, these honorable mentions represent a diverse golden age where automotive identity was forged through bold styling and mechanical character.
